Period terraces, Edwardians and bungalows suit made to measure joinery rather than a flat pack run, so the design discussion is likely to focus on finish style and how cabinetry sits with existing character. That is a different exercise from a compact kitchen where every storage decision affects bench space and circulation.

For apartment work, the guide flags owners corporation documentation, acoustic underlay and clever storage as real parts of the job. Those details are worth raising at the first visit, because they can affect what is practical, what needs approval and how smoothly installation can proceed.

Timing depends on fabrication and site readiness

The published timing sequence is specific enough to test competing promises. For a mid range full renovation, the guide says to allow roughly 6 to 12 weeks from contract signing to handover. It attributes the front half largely to cabinetry and stone fabrication, which take around 4 to 6 weeks as a lead in, followed by 2 to 4 weeks of site work and a further 1 to 2 weeks for finishing.

If a quote suggests a shorter program, ask whether appliances have already been chosen, whether cabinetry production has been allowed for, and when the site can actually be ready for demolition and installation.

Use the consult to test contract detail

The four step process described in the guide moves from form submission to callback, then an in home design consult, then build and handover. The practical payoff comes from what you confirm during the visit.

The published inclusions mention a fixed price written contract, a transparent variations process and domestic building insurance where the contract value requires it, so the consult should be used to pin down scope, assumptions and any items that may trigger change later. Hardware is another useful checkpoint.

Comparing quotes and scope

One proposal may assume the existing footprint stays in place, while another may include demolition, manufacture and installation for a full rebuild. Asking each renovator to price the same written scope is usually more useful than debating whether one total looks high or low in isolation.

State whether the layout stays as is or changes. List the finishes you already know you want. Note whether the home is a period property or an apartment. Ask for the quote in fixed price written form.

  1. Define the scope. Write down layout intent, rough budget, home type and must keep features before requesting a quote.
  2. Test the timeline. Ask whether appliances are chosen, cabinetry production is allowed for, and when the site is ready for demolition.
  3. Check contract detail. Confirm a fixed price written contract, a transparent variations process and domestic building insurance where required.
  4. Verify hardware. Check the specified brand and whether soft close is built into the base price, such as Blum or Hettich.
  5. Bring supporting material. Bring photos, rough measurements and a target budget to the first in home consult.

Common questions

What is a butler's pantry and is it worth it? A butler's pantry is a secondary prep and storage room behind the main kitchen, often with a second sink and bench. It keeps the main kitchen clear for entertaining and is one of the most requested features on larger Melbourne renovations. Whether it suits depends on your floor plan and budget, so the renovator can model it at the design stage.

Are soft-close drawers and doors standard? Most quality Melbourne kitchen renovations include soft-close runners and hinges, such as Blum or Hettich, as standard. Confirm the hardware brand and whether soft-close is included in the base price when you compare quotes.

Do you do small and apartment kitchen renovations? Yes, compact galley, single-wall and apartment kitchens are a common request across inner Melbourne. Your renovator can plan storage and bench space for tight footprints, including owners corporation requirements where they apply.
